Evaluative Language

A guide to evaluative meaning in discourse & applied linguistics

Definition Core Functions Main Categories Real‑world Examples Appraisal Theory Further Reading Resources

1. Definition｜定义

Evaluative language (also called evaluative discourse) refers to linguistic resources that speakers/writers use to express subjective judgements, attitudes, feelings, approval or disapproval towards entities, events, behaviours or other people.

"Evaluation is the broad cover‑term for the expression of the speaker’s or writer’s attitude or stance towards, viewpoint on, or feelings about the entities or propositions that he or she is talking about." — Hunston & Thompson (2000)

Distinction: Fact vs Evaluation

  • Fact (descriptive): The film lasted 120 minutes. (objective, measurable)
  • Evaluation (evaluative): This is a brilliant film. (subjective judgement)

2. Core Functions｜核心功能

  1. Express attitude: convey personal feelings, likes and dislikes.
  2. Build solidarity: align readers/listeners with the speaker’s position, build shared values.
  3. Persuade: influence audience opinions, used in advertising, politics and argumentative writing.
  4. Construct stance: mark how committed the author is to a claim (hedging / boosting).
  5. Signal value hierarchies: tell audiences what is good/bad, acceptable/unacceptable.

3. Main Categories｜主要分类

Category Description Typical Linguistic Forms
Affect（情感） Express emotional responses happy, terrifying, delighted, upset, amazing
Judgement（评判） Evaluate human behaviour (morality, skill, normality) clever, dishonest, lazy, heroic, reckless
Appreciation（鉴赏） Evaluate objects, art, events, performance elegant, ugly, well‑designed, chaotic, harmonious

These three categories are the foundation of Martin & White’s Appraisal Theory.

Other evaluative devices

  • Evaluative adjectives & adverbs: wonderfully, poorly, dangerously
  • Evaluative nouns: disaster, success, masterpiece, failure
  • Hedging words: perhaps, maybe, arguably, somewhat
  • Boosters: definitely, absolutely, obviously
  • Implied / indirect evaluation (no explicit value words, e.g. loaded descriptions)

4. Real‑world Examples｜实例

① Media & News
Sentence: The reckless policy brought severe harm to local residents.
Analysis: reckless (judgement), severe harm (negative appreciation/judgement).
② Advertising
Sentence: Experience this wonderful, revolutionary new skincare product.
Analysis: wonderful, revolutionary — positive affect & appreciation for persuasion.
③ Academic writing
Sentence: This flawed methodology leads to questionable conclusions.
Analysis: cautious negative evaluation in scholarly discourse.
④ Everyday conversation
Sentence: It was such a frustrating meeting.
Analysis: affect (negative emotion).

⚠️ Explicit vs Implicit Evaluation

  • Explicit（显性）: direct evaluative vocabulary. e.g. The speech was terrible.
  • Implicit（隐性）: no obvious evaluative words; evaluation is triggered by context.
    e.g. He arrived one hour late again. (listener infers negative judgement)

5. Connection with Appraisal Theory｜与评价理论的联系

Appraisal Theory (Martin & White, 2005) is the most influential systemic‑functional framework for analysing evaluative language. Its three subsystems:

  • ATTITUDE: Affect / Judgement / Appreciation (the core evaluative resources)
  • ENGAGEMENT: how speakers negotiate voices (monogloss / heterogloss; hedging, disclaiming, proclaiming)
  • GRADUATION: scaling up or down evaluation (force: raise/lower intensity; focus: sharpen/blur category boundaries)

Evaluative language is the broader phenomenon; Appraisal Theory is an analytical toolkit to dissect it.
